# 03. UX and Interaction Design

## 1. Principles

1. **Calm and predictable.** Muted colours; no sudden sound, flashing or pop-ups on the subject panel. Motion only where it carries meaning: gestures and signs, and a short rise as new pictures appear so the subject notices them.
2. **Pictures first.** The subject panel works for someone who cannot read.
3. **Officer in control.** Nothing reaches the subject without an officer action (FR-15).
4. **Always a fallback.** Every output has a simpler alternative, down to the officer performing the gesture.
5. **Large targets, high contrast.** For stress, shaking hands, sunlight and small screens.
6. **Trauma-aware wording.** Short, gentle, non-leading; safety and comfort first, sensitive questions never first (FR-12).

## 2. States

The phone is never split between two people. It is either with the officer or facing the subject.

**Show state: the stage** (phone facing the subject, pinned). The stage is the app's home while a session runs (D-20).

```
+--------------------------------+
|   ( "Do you want water?" )  (o)|  speech bubble in subject language; officer handle
|        \                       |
|      [ character ]   [water]   |  child or adult character performs the gesture; picture held up
| [>][½x]                        |  replay and slow (FR-18)
|--------------------------------|
| +----------------------------+ |
| |   camera mirror of their   | |  live front camera, nothing stored (FR-69)
| |   hands                    | |
| | [water] [food] [rest]      | |  choices, at most 6; numbers as a pad
| +----------------------------+ |
| [  ✓ YES  ]    [  ✗ NO  ]      |  always present, same place
| [ ? Don't know ] [ ✋ Stop ]   |  always offered (FR-70)
+--------------------------------+
```

**Officer state: the panel** (phone held by the officer; reached from the stage by holding the officer handle). Laid out like a messaging app, one screen, no page scroll.

```
+--------------------------------+
| (face) Child · Hindi  [☎][≡][⋮]|  profile and language, call, log, menu
|--------------------------------|
|        [ Are you hurt?      ]  |  what was shown, on the right
| (☺)[✔ Yes]                     |  the subject's answer, with its picture
|        [ Do you feel safe?  ]  |
|--------------------------------|
| (Safety)(Health)(Needs)(Who).. |  triage groups; sensitive group locked
| [Hurt? ✋🔊] [Water? ✋] [...]   |  suggestion cards: what the subject will get
| Ready to show: Hurt? ✓✓✓   [x] |  the prepared item and its media
| (+) [Type anything...   ] (➤)  |  + opens gesture lookup, pictures, draw, language
+--------------------------------+
```

Send shows the prepared item or the typed text at once. While typing, the suggestions give way to the Signs, Gestures, Spell and Pictures toggles.

**Home** (before a session): the character greets in the chosen language; four faces choose the profile (young child, signs, other language, not sure); one language picker; Start. Starting shows the opening greeting on the stage, or the language finder for someone who speaks another language.

- **Rotate** turns the Show state 180 degrees for a subject sitting opposite (FR-02).
- Returning to the Officer state is one tap on the officer handle, small and quiet in the top corner. Back does nothing on the stage. A department can also require the fingerprint or device PIN (setting "Screen lock to leave the stage"). The app stays pinned for the whole session, so the subject cannot reach other apps either (FR-61, D-23).
- Every answer gets the same small acknowledgement (a nod and "Okay"), whatever it is, so the character never rewards one answer over another.
- "Stop" ends nothing by itself; it tells the officer, with a banner, to pause and check the person is comfortable.
- The Show state never displays notes, the log, or anything typed but not confirmed.

## 3. Subject profiles

Chosen on the start screen with one tap and changeable from the officer bar (FR-60).

| Profile | Show state content | Audio |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Young child | Picture cards, child character gesture card or clip, short caption | Recorded audio in the subject language, low volume |
| ISL user | Picture cards, caption, human ISL clip or adult character phrase | Off |
| Hearing, other language | Language finder first; then picture cards and caption | Recorded audio in the chosen language |
| Not sure | Picture cards, Yes and No, drawing | Off; officer can play audio per question |

## 4. Key flows

**Start.** Unlock, choose profile and subject language. For *Hearing, other language*, the first Show screen is the language finder: tiles in each language's own script; tapping a tile plays its greeting; the subject's choice sets the language (FR-63).

**Preset question.** Tap a group, tap a question, tap Show. The subject sees the content for the profile within 300 ms. The log records "shown" with the time.

**Free text.** Type in the composer at the bottom of the officer panel, set the toggles (Signs, Gestures, Spell, Pictures), tap Send. The stage shows the caption with the current word lit; the character signs or gestures each known word, fingerspells the rest with the current letter shown beside it, and holds up each word's picture. Answer pictures can be attached first from the + tray (FR-13, D-22).

**Phrase.** Open the *Phrases* group, pick a sentence pattern ("Do you want ...?") and then the picture it is about (water). The subject sees the whole sentence as a caption with the picture held up, and the character signs its parts in order as one movement (FR-62).

**Gesture lookup and show-back.** Tap *They gestured*. Tap where the hand went on a body outline, then what it did (point, touch, stroke, circle, flat hand, fist, both hands). The app lists matching meanings with picture and clip. Pick the likely one and tap Show: the subject sees the gesture and picture with "Is this it?" and Yes/No. Yes logs the meaning; No or no answer logs "unclear" (FR-65, FR-66).

**Drawing.** In the Show state the subject taps *draw*, draws with a finger, and taps *done* (or *start again*). The drawing is logged as the answer and the stage returns to the character's greeting until the officer takes the phone back.

**Interpreter.** Tap *Call* in the officer bar. The messaging app opens to the state's interpreter contact; the officer starts the video call there (FR-64).

**End.** Menu, *End session*. The officer chooses *Export report*, *End without export* or *Cancel*. Export shows the password once, then offers *Share report* and *Finish and delete session*. Ending is final and says so.

## 5. Accessibility

Applies GIGW 3.0 and IS 17802 (WCAG 2.1 AA) to the officer panel and stricter rules to the subject panel.

| Area | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Targets | 48 dp minimum; subject cards 96 dp minimum (NFR-20) |
| Contrast | 4.5:1 text, 3:1 icons and card borders (NFR-21) |
| Colour | Yes and No also differ by icon and position |
| Text size | Officer panel follows the system font scale up to 200% (NFR-22) |
| Screen reader | Officer controls labelled; TalkBack order follows visual order (NFR-23) |
| Motion | Clips start only on officer action; no autoplay loops; "remove animations" respected for non-sign motion |
| Sound | Per profile (§3); never sudden; officer can mute |
| Scripts | All text from resources; Baloo 2 is bundled for Latin and Devanagari headings and captions; every other scheduled script uses the system Noto fonts; layouts tested with the longest translation |

## 6. Visual style

- The stage is a warm room (peach sky, soft floor, low sun); the officer side is cool slate paper with an indigo gradient header and main buttons, white cards with soft shadows (radius 20), 14-radius buttons and fields, and pill-shaped chips, so the two states are never confused.
- Rounded, soft shapes throughout; subject buttons squash gently and give a short haptic tick when pressed, so a child, including a deaf child, sees and feels the tap land.
- Yes is green with a tick, No is coral with a cross, "I don't know" lavender, "Stop" amber with a raised hand.
- Baloo 2 for headings and captions; the system sans-serif for officer body text.
- Cards are flat illustrations: no photographs of real people, no weapons, injuries or frightening imagery; police and medical cards drawn friendly; diverse skin tones, dress and regions.
- Dark theme for the officer panel at night; the subject panel stays calm in both themes.

## 7. Content style

| Do | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| "I am a police officer. I am here to help you." | "You are safe now" and other promises the officer may not be able to keep |
| "Are you hurt?" | "Did someone hurt you?" as an early question |
| "Can you show me?" | Leading questions that suggest an answer |
| One idea per question, present tense | Double questions ("Are you hungry or thirsty?") |

Every subject-facing string, translation, card, clip and audio file passes the [content policy](governance/policies/content-policy.md) review before release.
