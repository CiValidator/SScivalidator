# 03. UX and Interaction Design

## 1. Principles

1. **Calm and predictable.** Muted colours; no sudden sound, flashing or pop-ups on the subject panel. Motion only where it carries meaning: gestures and signs, and a short rise as new pictures appear so the subject notices them.
2. **Pictures first.** The subject panel works for someone who cannot read.
3. **Officer in control.** Nothing reaches the subject without an officer action.
4. **Always a fallback.** Every output has a simpler alternative, down to the officer performing the gesture.
5. **Large targets, high contrast.** For stress, shaking hands, sunlight and small screens.
6. **Trauma-aware wording.** Short, gentle, non-leading; safety and comfort first, sensitive questions never first.

## 2. States

The phone is never split between two people. It is either with the officer or facing the subject.

**Show state: the stage** (phone facing the subject, pinned). The stage is the app's home while a session runs.

A plain line over the top of the camera window says what to do now ("Tap your answer, or sign it to the camera", or "Tap Next when you are ready"). The introduction and ground rules show only Next and Stop; the practice round has no Yes, No or "That's wrong".

The answers sit in two narrow rails (buttons 56 dp tall) either side of a camera window drawn at the camera's own 9:16 shape and fitted, not cropped: the subject sees everything the camera sees. Draw, Write and the camera button use neutral tool colours so Stop stands alone. Write and the camera button are not offered to a young child.

For a lesson-sheet question the panel under the character becomes its input instead: a number or phone pad, a name or place field, a class grid, a state and district picker, or several pictures with Done, with "I don't know", "I don't understand", "That's wrong", the camera button and Stop in a row beneath. A written or typed answer is read back large with "Is this right?"; only the subject's Yes makes it theirs. A button by the character opens the phrase board of everyday phrases.

While the subject signs, the rails hold only Stop and Done, a 20-second bar runs, "Sign to the camera. Tap Done when you finish." shows, and one dot appears per sign seen; the reader's guesses are never shown as words. When Done ends a short answer, the stage at once shows two or three pictures per sign and "None of these", and only the person's pick records a meaning. After a failed read of lost or fast hands, "A little slower, please" appears with a picture for about three seconds. **Tell me** shows "I'm watching. Take your time." with no timer, only Stop and Done. When the officer taps **Ask them to choose**, the stage shows two or three picture cards for each sign and "None of these".

After Stop, a night-sky pause layer with a breathing circle and bubbles to pop covers the whole stage, with no score and no sound; only the subject's **I'm ready** ends it. In glare the stage turns black, white and yellow (sun mode).

**Officer state: the panel** (phone held by the officer; reached from the stage by one tap on the officer handle). Laid out like a messaging app in two pages: **Transcribe** (shown below) and **More** (every question group and phrase, They gestured, the picture board, Draw, the language finder, Tell me, the ISL dictionary, the session log with notes, profile, role and language). Picking something on More returns to Transcribe.

Send shows the prepared item or the typed text at once. While typing, the suggestions give way to the Signs, Gestures, Spell and Pictures toggles.

**Home** (before a session): the character greets in the chosen language; the role and the profile (young child, signs, other language, not sure) are chosen side by side as equal-height tiles; one language picker; Start. Starting shows the opening greeting on the stage, or the language finder for someone who speaks another language.

- **Rotate** turns the Show state 180 degrees for a subject sitting opposite.
- Showing can also be done by tipping the phone over its top edge toward the subject (about 0.6 s) or with Volume Up. On the stage, single volume presses do nothing; holding both for 1.5 s returns to the officer.
- Returning to the Officer state is one tap on the officer handle, small and quiet in the top corner. Back does nothing on the stage. A department can also require the fingerprint or device PIN (setting "Screen lock to leave the stage"). The app stays pinned for the whole session, so the subject cannot reach other apps either.
- Every answer gets the same small acknowledgement (a nod and "Okay"), whatever it is, so the character never rewards one answer over another.
- "Stop" ends nothing by itself; it opens the pause layer for the subject and tells the officer, with a banner, to pause and check the person is comfortable.
- Whoever holds the phone feels the answer: one long buzz for Yes, two for No, three short pulses for Stop, Break, "I don't know", "I don't understand" or "That's wrong", and a tick for a picture; never for a young child.
- The Show state never displays notes, the log, or anything typed but not confirmed.

## 3. Subject profiles

Chosen on the start screen with one tap and changeable from the officer bar.

| Profile | Show state content | Audio |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Young child | Picture cards, child character gesture card or clip, short caption | Recorded audio in the subject language, low volume |
| ISL user | Picture cards, caption, human ISL clip or adult character phrase | Off |
| Hearing, other language | Language finder first; then picture cards and caption | Recorded audio in the chosen language |
| Not sure | Picture cards, Yes and No, drawing | Off; officer can play audio per question |

## 4. Key flows

**Start.** Unlock, choose profile, role (or leave "Not known yet") and subject language. The session opens with the officer's introduction, four ground rules ("I was not there", "you can say I don't know", "tell me if I'm wrong", "you can take a break") and a practice round ("Show me what you did this morning"), each moved on by the subject's **Next**. For *Hearing, other language*, the first Show screen is the language finder: tiles in each language's own script; tapping a tile plays its greeting; the subject's choice sets the language.

**Preset question.** Tap a group, tap a question, tap Show. The subject sees the content for the profile within 300 ms. The log records "shown" with the time.

**Free text.** Type in the composer at the bottom of the officer panel, set the toggles (Signs, Gestures, Spell, Pictures), tap Send. The stage shows the caption with the current word lit; the character signs or gestures each known word, fingerspells the rest with the current letter shown beside it, and holds up each word's picture. Answer pictures can be attached first from the + tray.

**Phrase.** Open the *Phrases* group, pick a sentence pattern ("Do you want ...?") and then the picture it is about (water). The subject sees the whole sentence as a caption with the picture held up, and the character signs its parts in order as one movement.

**Gesture lookup and show-back.** Tap *They gestured*. Tap where the hand went on a body outline, then what it did (point, touch, stroke, circle, flat hand, fist, both hands). The app lists matching meanings with picture and clip. Pick the likely one and tap Show: the subject sees the gesture and picture with "Is this it?" and Yes/No. Yes logs the meaning; No or no answer logs "unclear".

**Sign an answer.** The officer taps **Read signs** (or the subject taps the camera button). The character keeps showing the question; the subject signs; Done or a 20-second limit ends the window, and the stage asks the person to pick what each sign meant. The officer sees the app's reading per sign ("read by the app, not checked") and can replay the signing at ¼× or ½× with sign markers, type a word per sign with the reader's top three guesses as chips, and save it as the officer's reading. **Ask them to choose** shows the subject two or three pictures per sign plus "None of these"; only their tap records a meaning.

**Personal details.** On More, open Personal, Family, Address or School and show a question ("What is your name?"). The character signs it in draft ISL order and the stage opens its input. The subject enters the answer, sees it read back and taps Yes; the conversation shows the value with how it was given, and the report lists it under "Personal details given".

**ISL dictionary.** On More, **ISL dictionary** lists every recorded and draft sign by category; search in English or Hindi, tap a word to see the sign on the officer's screen, and **Show to the person** prepares it for the stage.

**Tell me.** On More, **Tell me** opens a 3-minute window for an account. The officer or an interpreter replays it later in the session; nothing is kept after it ends.

**Say it back.** Under any answer, the officer taps **Say it back**, edits their reading if needed, and shows it. Where the item has other pictures, the subject picks among them; their pick makes the line Confirmed, and "No" marks it "Said no".

**Question run.** Long-press quick questions to queue them, or tap **Safety basics**: up to five go on one handover, each 1.4 s after the previous answer. Stop, Something else once every picture has been seen, or taking the phone back ends the run.

**Auto.** With Auto on, a card proposes the next welfare item and why ("Welfare · step 3 of 10"). The officer approves, skips or chooses something else; only statements may follow statements on the subject's Next. Stop pauses Auto; Resume asks "Can we carry on?"; a second Stop ends Auto.

**Rights.** For an accused, the rights groups appear. Each item shows the officer a card: rights must also be explained through a qualified interpreter, and the record says shown, not understood. The subject sees the same line under the caption.

**Second screen.** Menu, **Second screen**: pick a device paired in Bluetooth settings, type the 6-digit code it shows, check that both screens show the same check number, and the stage taps Match. A bar shows the device, its battery and the last answer. If the link is lost the officer gets **Reconnect** and **Use this phone**.

**Drawing.** In the Show state the subject taps *draw*, picks an ink colour (dark, red, blue, green or brown), draws with a finger, takes back a line with *undo* or everything with *start again*, and taps *done*. The drawing is logged as the answer. If it answered a question, the stage returns to that question; if the officer asked for a drawing, the stage thanks the person and asks them to give the phone back, with *draw more* to add to it. The officer sees the drawing in the conversation and can open it full size.

**Interpreter.** Tap *Call* in the officer bar. The messaging app opens to the state's interpreter contact; the officer starts the video call there.

**Finish.** *Finish session* at the foot of More or in the menu. A summary shows the time taken, what was shown and answered, and anything left open; *Thank them on the stage* closes the conversation for the person. The officer names the session and taps *Save and finish* or *Export report* (the password is shown once, then *Share report* and *Done*). *Delete without saving* needs a second tap. Saved sessions are listed on the start screen to open again or delete. Back never finishes a session: it returns to the start screen with the session still open.

## 5. Accessibility

Applies GIGW 3.0 and IS 17802 (WCAG 2.1 AA) to the officer panel and stricter rules to the subject panel.

| Area | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Targets | 48 dp minimum; subject cards 96 dp minimum |
| Contrast | 4.5:1 text, 3:1 icons and card borders |
| Colour | Yes and No also differ by icon and position |
| Text size | Officer panel follows the system font scale up to 200% |
| Screen reader | Officer controls labelled; TalkBack order follows visual order |
| Motion | Clips start only on officer action; no autoplay loops; "remove animations" respected for non-sign motion |
| Glare | Sun mode switches the stage to black, white and yellow above 10,000 lux |
| Touch | TalkBack users keep normal volume keys; tilt and volume shortcuts can be turned off |
| Sound | Per profile (§3); never sudden; officer can mute |
| Scripts | All text from resources; Baloo 2 is bundled for Latin and Devanagari headings and captions; every other scheduled script uses the system Noto fonts; layouts tested with the longest translation |

## 6. Visual style

- The stage is a warm room (peach sky, soft floor, low sun); the officer side is cool slate paper with an indigo gradient header and main buttons, one card style (white, 1 dp outline instead of a shadow, radius 20, so it holds in dark theme and sunlight), one choice-tile style (tinted fill, 2 dp border and a check when selected), 14-radius buttons and fields, pill-shaped chips, and spacing tokens of 4, 8, 12, 16, 24 and 32 dp, so the two states are never confused.
- The character is framed from the head to the hips, so both hands stay in view at rest and at waist level.
- Rounded, soft shapes throughout; subject buttons squash gently and give a short haptic tick when pressed, so a child, including a deaf child, sees and feels the tap land.
- Yes is green with a tick, No is coral with a cross, "I don't know", "I don't understand" and "That's wrong" lavender; "Stop" is an octagonal stop sign. Thumb-up, thumb-down and open palm are never used for yes, no or stop.
- Baloo 2 for headings and captions; the system sans-serif for officer body text.
- Cards are flat illustrations: no photographs of real people, no weapons, injuries or frightening imagery; police and medical cards drawn friendly; diverse skin tones, dress and regions.
- Dark theme for the officer panel at night; the subject panel stays calm in both themes.

## 7. Content style

| Do | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| "I am a police officer. I am here to help you." | "You are safe now" and other promises the officer may not be able to keep |
| "Are you hurt?" | "Did someone hurt you?" as an early question, or any question that presupposes a person did something (ABE 3.71) |
| "Can you show me?" | Leading questions that suggest an answer |
| One idea per question, present tense | Double questions ("Are you hungry or thirsty?") |

Every subject-facing string, translation, card, clip and audio file passes the content policy review before release.
