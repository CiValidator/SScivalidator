# Privacy Notice: Sign Setu

*Draft for legal review. Placeholders in angle brackets are filled in by the department.*

Sign Setu is an app used by officers of <Department name> to communicate with people who cannot communicate in words, such as people who are deaf, mute or in distress, when the police find or help them.

## What data the app handles

- The questions and pictures the officer shows.
- The answers given on the phone: Yes, No, I don't know, I don't understand, That's wrong, Stop, a picture, a drawing, writing, or a gesture or sign whose meaning the person picks.
- Whether the person is a victim or witness or an accused person, once the officer knows it.
- Notes the officer writes.
- The kind of help needed (for example, young child or sign language user) and the language chosen. No name is needed.
- The camera: while a question is on the screen, the front camera shows the person their own hands, like a mirror. The pictures are not recorded, stored or sent anywhere. The officer can switch the mirror off for a person, and the department can switch it off for all officers.
- Signing: only when the officer or the person starts a sign window (up to 20 seconds for an answer, up to 3 minutes for an account), the phone works out points on the hands, arms and face to read the signs. These points are biometric data. They stay in the phone's memory during the conversation so an interpreter can replay the signing, and are wiped when the conversation ends or the app locks. They are never saved, sent or used to identify anyone.
- A second screen: if the officer uses a department tablet or phone as the person's screen, answers travel encrypted over Bluetooth to the officer's phone; the second screen keeps nothing.

The app does not use the microphone or location, and does not ask for names, addresses, photographs or identity documents.

## Where it goes

- Conversation records stay on the officer's phone, encrypted, and are deleted when the officer ends the conversation or automatically after <24 hours>.
- This version of the app has no internet access. Nothing is sent to any server or service.
- If needed, the officer exports a password-protected record of the conversation to the official case file, which is handled under the rules for police records.
- If the officer calls an interpreter, the call takes place in the department's messaging app (<Sandes or WhatsApp>) under that app's terms. Sign Setu passes no details of the conversation to it.

## Why

To keep the person safe, understand their immediate needs and help reunite them with their family, as part of the police's duties under law, including the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act 2015 for children.

## Children

Information about children is handled with extra care: it is never used for tracking, profiling or advertising, and the identity of a child is never disclosed.

## Security

The app encrypts data on the phone, opens only with the officer's fingerprint or phone PIN, blocks screenshots, keeps the screen fixed to the app while it faces the person, and is security-audited by a CERT-In empanelled auditor.
