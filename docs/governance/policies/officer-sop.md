# Officer Operating Procedure: Sign Setu

For police officers using the app in the field. Keep it short in training: the quick card summarises sections 2 to 5.

## 1. What the app is, and is not

- It **is** a communication aid for the first conversation with a person who cannot speak or hear, or is too distressed to speak.
- It **is not** an interpreter, a counsellor, or a way to record a statement. The meaning of a gesture or sign can be misread, so the person always confirms it. Every export is a communication note, not a statement: a statement needs a qualified interpreter and the statutory video recording (BNSS s.173 and s.183(6), BSA s.125).
- For a child, follow the Juvenile Justice Act procedure and the missing and found children SOP. For any sexual offence, follow the POCSO Act: statements are taken with an interpreter or special educator and recorded as the Act requires. Use the app only to keep the person safe and comfortable until then.

## 2. Before you start

1. Make sure you are somewhere the person feels safe, away from crowds where possible.
2. Sit or crouch at their level.
3. Choose who you are helping and their language, then start the session. When you tap **Show to them**, turn the phone so it faces them; the app stays on that screen until you take it back by tapping the small lock.
4. If they sit opposite you, use the menu option **They sit opposite me** so the screen is the right way up for them.
5. Set the person's role only when you know it. For an accused, show the rights items, and arrange for the rights to be explained through a qualified interpreter; the app records them as shown, not understood. For anyone who may be under 18, follow the Juvenile Justice Act and mark them in the app.
6. Let the opening run: the ground rules tell the person they may say they do not know, correct you, or take a break.

## 3. Asking

1. Start with comfort and safety: "I am a police officer. I am here to help you", "Are you hurt?", "Do you want water?" Do not promise things you may not be able to keep, such as "You are safe now".
2. Ask one question at a time and wait. Do not rush.
3. Do not ask about what happened until the person is calm and safe. Sensitive questions are behind a second tap on purpose.
4. Do not ask leading questions ("Did he hit you?"). Use open questions or show choices.
5. If you type your own question, keep it short and use plain words: the character signs or spells it word by word, which is not full ISL grammar. Prefer a prepared question when one fits.
6. If they tap **Stop** or **Break**, pause. Check they are comfortable, offer water or a rest, and go on only when they tap **I'm ready**.
7. If your department allows **Auto**, it only suggests welfare questions; you still approve every one. Never use it to ask about what happened.

## 4. Understanding answers

1. Prefer picture and Yes/No answers; they are the most reliable.
2. Treat a gesture or sign as a clue, not a fact. The app's reading of signs and your own reading are readings. Use **Ask them to choose** or **Say it back**: only their pick counts.
3. Never ask a person to sign one sign at a time. If the app asks them to sign more slowly, that is after it failed to read fast hands; let them carry on.
4. Treat a signed account (**Tell me**) as a first account for safety and support only. Arrange an interpreter and the statutory recording for any statement.
5. Never take an important decision (where the person goes, who they are handed to) on a single gesture or answer. "I don't know" is a good answer; do not push.
6. If you are unsure, say so and wait for an interpreter.

## 5. Privacy

1. Do not type names, addresses or phone numbers unless they are needed now; put identity details in the official record.
2. Do not take screenshots or photos of the screen (the app blocks screenshots).
3. Do not show the screen to bystanders.
4. When finished, export to the official record if needed, then **End session**. Share the export password separately, never in the same message as the file.
5. If your phone is lost or stolen, tell your supervisor at once.

## 5a. Second screen

1. Use department devices only, renamed in their settings so they can be told apart.
2. On every stage device turn on **Ask for PIN before unpinning** and a short screen-lock time.
3. Pair the devices once in Bluetooth settings. Check that both screens show the same check number before the stage taps Match.
4. Keep the stage in your line of sight and the support person's. A child is never alone with it.
5. When the session ends, collect the stage device yourself; it stays on the hand-back screen until its own PIN is entered.
6. Paired mode does not change the app's status: it is never a statement.

## 6. When things fail

| Problem | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| The character does not move | Read the caption and point to pictures |
| The camera mirror shows a hand picture instead | The answer cards still work; use pictures, Yes/No or drawing |
| The app says an update is required | Finish the open session, then install the current version from your department |
| Person is too distressed | Stop using the app. Comfort them. Call for support (Childline 1098 for children). |

## 7. Things you must never do

- Use the app to interrogate anyone, or to ask an accused for an account of events.
- Use the app to record a statement or as evidence of what the person said.
- Share session content on WhatsApp, social media or personal storage.
